# source_policy.md

## Purpose
This file defines which sources the mathematics content agent is allowed to rely on.

## Principles
- Prefer authoritative mathematical sources.
- Prefer primary or institutional sources for historical/date claims when possible.
- Do not rely on random blogs or anonymous summaries.
- Important claims should be checked against at least two reliable sources whenever possible.

## Source Tiers

### Tier 1: Authoritative / Primary / Institutional
Use these first whenever available.
Examples:
- university archives
- official biography pages from reputable institutions
- official society pages
- original publication records
- publisher pages for classic works
- institutional history pages

### Tier 2: High-quality mathematical references
These are acceptable and often useful for verification.
Examples:
- recognized mathematical encyclopedias
- reputable mathematics history resources
- well-maintained educational institution pages
- trustworthy reference projects with named editorial control

### Tier 3: General background sources
These may help with orientation but should not be the sole basis for strong claims.
Examples:
- general encyclopedias
- reputable general-interest history summaries

## Prohibited source types
- anonymous blogs
- SEO content farms
- AI-generated pages with no editorial control
- meme pages
- social media reposts as factual evidence
- quote websites without verification
- unattributed image macros

## Rules
1. A core factual claim should ideally be supported by at least two reliable sources.
2. Date linkage must be explicitly supported.
3. A quote must be verifiable in wording and attribution.
4. If a source contradicts another on a key fact, flag the conflict.
5. If only weak sources are available, do not publish automatically.

## Operational guidance
- Store source title, URL, and notes.
- Store source tier.
- Keep raw evidence for audit purposes.
- Mark uncertain claims clearly in verification notes.
